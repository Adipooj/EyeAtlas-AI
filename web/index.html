◎ Module 2 Clinical Guide: Visual Field Perimetry (Supra-Threshold 24-2 & 30-2)

A. How to Use (Step-by-Step Clinical Procedure)

  1. Instruct the patient to keep their gaze strictly locked on the central yellow fixation cross at all times. Emphasize: "Do not look around for the dots; keep looking only at the central cross."
  2. Place Phone 2 in the patient's hands in Patient Response Pad Mode. The entire screen acts as a single large touch button.
  3. Tell the patient: "Whenever you notice a quick flash of light anywhere in your side vision, tap the screen once immediately."
  4. The native VR app presents Goldmann Size III spots (0.43° visual angle, 200 ms duration) at 54 standard grid points across the central 24° visual field.
  5. The software periodically presents stimuli into the physiological blind spot (15° temporal, 1.5° inferior) to monitor fixation losses (Heijl-Krakau technique), along with silent false-positive catch trials.

B. What It Screens For

  • Glaucomatous Field Loss: Arcuate scotomas, nasal steps, paracentral depressions.
  • Neurological Lesions: Bitemporal hemianopia (chiasm/pituitary), homonymous hemianopia (post-chiasmal/stroke).
  • Retinal Vascular Occlusions: Branch retinal vein / artery occlusion.
  • Retinitis Pigmentosa & Toxicity: Chloroquine / hydroxychloroquine toxicity.

◆ Module 1 Clinical Guide: Visual Acuity (ETDRS / LogMAR / Pinhole Differential)

A. How to Use (Step-by-Step Clinical Procedure)

  1. Ensure the patient is seated comfortably wearing the VR Box with IPD adjusted until the two eye views fuse into a single sharp field.
  2. Select Visual Acuity on the Clinician Station (Phone 2 or Web Portal) and choose the starting eye (Right Eye OD or Left Eye OS).
  3. The patient will see a high-contrast Tumbling "E" optotype in one of 4 orientations: Up (↑), Down (↓), Left (←), or Right (→).
  4. The patient indicates the orientation using the directional touch zones on the Patient Response Pad (Phone 2 browser) or states the direction verbally to the health worker.
  5. The software runs an adaptive 4-down / 1-up staircase from LogMAR 1.0 (Snellen 6/60) to LogMAR -0.1 (Snellen 6/4.8).
  6. Pinhole Differential Protocol: If the patient's baseline visual acuity is abnormal (≥ 0.3 LogMAR / worse than 6/12), place the 1.2 mm pinhole occluder over the tested eye and re-run the staircase.

B. What It Screens For

  • Refractive Errors: Myopia, Hyperopia, Astigmatism.
  • Amblyopia: Functional unilateral vision loss ("lazy eye").
  • Media Opacities: Cataract, corneal scars, vitreous haze.
  • Retinal / Macular Diseases: Macular Degeneration, Diabetic Maculopathy.

C. Diagnostic Interpretation Guide (What Every Reading Means)

LogMAR Score Snellen Metric Interpretation Clinical Screening Significance
≤ 0.00 6/6 (20/20) Normal Visual Acuity Normal central resolving power. Low risk of significant pathology.
0.10 to 0.20 6/7.5 to 6/9 Mild Visual Impairment Borderline acuity. Routine optometric evaluation recommended.
0.30 to 0.50 6/12 to 6/18 Moderate Impairment Meets threshold for driving restriction. Pinhole test mandatory.
0.60 to 0.90 6/24 to 6/48 Severe Impairment Substantial central vision loss. High suspicion of organic pathology.
≥ 1.00 ≤ 6/60 (20/200) Profound Impairment Legal blindness threshold. Urgent ophthalmology referral.
Pinhole Differential Rule:
• Pinhole Responsive (≥ 2 LogMAR lines improvement): Uncorrected Refractive Error (optical defocus neutralized). Non-urgent spectacle refraction.
• Pinhole Refractory (No improvement or worsens): Organic Pathology (cataract, corneal scar, macular lesion, amblyopia). Urgent ophthalmology referral required.

◘ Module 3 Clinical Guide: Colour Vision (Calibrated 24-Plate Ishihara Battery)

A. How to Use (Step-by-Step Clinical Procedure)

  1. Ensure the patient display is set to standard sRGB colour space with neutral white balance (6500 K) and photopic luminance.
  2. Select Colour Vision in either Monocular Mode (testing each eye independently) or Stereoscopic VR Mode.
  3. Plates 1 through 24 are sequentially presented for 3 to 5 seconds each:
    • Plate 1 (Demo Plate 12): Seen by all functional eyes; instructs patient.
    • Plates 2–7 (Transformation Plates): Normal sees one number; defective sees a different number.
    • Plates 8–13 (Vanishing Plates): Normal sees number; defective sees nothing.

  • Congenital Colour Deficiency: Protanopia / Protanomaly (red), Deuteranopia / Deuteranomaly (green).
  • Acquired Optic Nerve Pathology: Optic Neuritis, Ethambutol toxic optic neuropathy, early Compressive Neuropathy.
  • Macular Dystrophies: Cone-rod dystrophy, Stargardt disease.

◑ Module 4 Clinical Guide: Contrast Sensitivity (Pelli-Robson LogCS Steps)

A. How to Use (Step-by-Step Clinical Procedure)

  1. In the VR headset, present triplet optotype letters at a fixed spatial frequency (1.5 cycles/degree, optotype size equivalent to 6/60).
  2. The optotype contrast progressively diminishes across 16 triplet steps, decreasing by 0.15 log units per triplet from 0.00 LogCS (100% contrast) down to 2.25 LogCS (0.56% contrast).
  3. The patient reads or selects the letters on Phone 2.
  4. The screening threshold is established at the lowest contrast triplet where the patient correctly identifies at least 2 out of 3 letters.

B. What It Screens For

  • Early Glaucoma: Parvocellular/magnocellular contrast loss often precedes visual field scotomas.
  • Optic Neuritis & MS: Demyelinating optic neuropathy causes profound contrast depression even with 6/6 Snellen acuity.
  • Early Cataract: Diffuse forward light scatter degrades retinal image contrast.
  • Diabetic Retinopathy: Macular ischemia and capillary non-perfusion.

C. Diagnostic Interpretation Guide (What Every Reading Means)

LogCS Value Contrast Threshold (%) Clinical Screening Significance
≥ 1.80 < 1.6% Normal Contrast Sensitivity: Healthy ocular media, clear optical pathway.
1.50 to 1.79 1.6% - 3.2% Mild Contrast Loss: Early subtle media changes (cataract), early glaucoma, mild DR.
1.20 to 1.49 3.2% - 6.3% Moderate Loss: Significant difficulty with night driving, steps, and low-contrast reading.
< 1.20 > 6.3% Severe Impairment: High risk of mobility accidents and falls. Urgent evaluation indicated.

▣ Module 5 Clinical Guide: Central Amsler Grid (10° Metamorphopsia & Macular Mapping)

A. How to Use (Step-by-Step Clinical Procedure)

  1. Display a white grid pattern with a central fixation dot on a black background covering the central 20° of vision (10° around fovea).
  2. The test is strictly monocular (tested OD, then OS, with non-tested eye occluded).
  3. The patient fixes their gaze steadily on the central white dot.
  4. While looking directly at the center, the patient reports:
    • "Are any of the straight lines appearing wavy, bent, or distorted?" (Metamorphopsia).
    • "Are any areas of the grid blurred, darkened, or completely missing?" (Scotoma).
  5. The patient or clinician traces the distorted/dark zones on the interactive touchscreen grid.

B. What It Screens For

  • Age-Related Macular Degeneration (AMD): Wet neovascular AMD and Dry geographic atrophy.
  • Diabetic Macular Edema (DME): Retinal thickening disrupting photoreceptor alignment.
  • Central Serous Chorioretinopathy (CSCR): Subretinal neurosensory detachment.
  • Macular Hole & Epiretinal Membrane: Vitreomacular traction and macular pucker.

C. Diagnostic Interpretation Guide (What Every Reading Means)

◔ Module 6 Clinical Guide: Binocular Vision & Alignment (Worth 4-Dot & Hirschberg)

A. How to Use (Step-by-Step Clinical Procedure)

  1. Worth 4-Dot Test:
    • In the VR headset, dichoptically present 4 targets: 1 Red (top), 2 Green (left and right), 1 White (bottom).
    • The right eye sees red targets; left eye sees green targets; both see the bottom white target.
    • The patient reports the dot count and colours: "2 Red", "3 Green", "4 Dots", or "5 Dots".
  2. Hirschberg Corneal Reflex Test:
    • Direct the penlight at the patient's nose bridge from 33 cm while fixating on the light.
    • Observe the position of the light reflex on both corneas relative to the pupil center.

B. What It Screens For

  • Strabismus: Esotropia (inward), Exotropia (outward), Hypertropia (vertical).
  • Foveal Suppression: Cortical sensory suppression to prevent diplopia.
  • Amblyogenic Risk: Suppression amblyopia in young patients.
  • Pathological Diplopia: Acquired double vision from cranial nerve paresis.

C. Diagnostic Interpretation Guide (What Every Reading Means)

◚ Module 7 Clinical Guide: Ocular Motility (9 Cardinal Gazes & Smooth Pursuit)

A. How to Use (Step-by-Step Clinical Procedure)

  1. Smooth Pursuit: In the VR headset, a moving target glides horizontally and vertically in a smooth sinusoidal pattern at 15°/s.
  2. 9 Cardinal Gazes: The target pauses at 9 anatomical gaze endpoints:
    • Primary Position (straight ahead).
    • Dextroversion (right) & Levoversion (left).
    • Sursumversion (up) & Deorsumversion (down).
    • Dextroelevation (up-right) & Dextrodepression (down-right).
    • Levoelevation (up-left) & Levodepression (down-left).
  3. The clinician inspects for conjugate movement, limitation of movement, pain on movement, or end-point nystagmus.

B. What It Screens For

  • CN III (Oculomotor): Inability to look up, down, or in; accompanied by ptosis and dilated pupil.
  • CN IV (Trochlear): Inability to look down-and-in; vertical diplopia with head tilt.
  • CN VI (Abducens): Inability to abduct (look outwards); horizontal diplopia.
  • Graves' Orbitopathy: Fibrotic restriction of inferior / medial rectus.
  • Internuclear Ophthalmoplegia (INO): Medial longitudinal fasciculus lesion (MS / stroke).

◉ Module 8 Clinical Guide: Pupillary Assessment & RAPD (Swinging Flashlight)

A. How to Use (Step-by-Step Clinical Procedure)

  1. Perform the examination in a dimly lit, dark-adapted room.
  2. Instruct the patient to fixate on a distant target to avoid near-effort accommodative miosis.
  3. Direct Light Reflex: Shine penlight directly into the right eye from a 45° angle. Observe for brisk constriction. Repeat left.
  4. Consensual Light Reflex: Shine into right eye while observing left pupil. It must constrict symmetrically. Repeat opposite.
  5. Swinging Flashlight Test for RAPD:
    • Swing the penlight rapidly back and forth between eyes, holding 2–3 seconds on each eye.
    • Observe the initial reaction of each pupil when the light strikes it.
    • Normal response: immediate bilateral pupil constriction under each swing.

B. What It Screens For

  • Relative Afferent Pupillary Defect (RAPD / Marcus Gunn Pupil): Asymmetric optic nerve lesion.
  • Optic Nerve Diseases: Optic Neuritis, Ischemic Optic Neuropathy (AION), Advanced Glaucoma, Traumatic Neuropathy.
  • Extensive Retinal Pathology: Central Retinal Artery Occlusion (CRAO), Ischemic CRVO, Massive Retinal Detachment.
  • Horner's Syndrome & Third Nerve Palsy: Anisocoria and sympathetic/parasympathetic denervation.

C. Diagnostic Interpretation Guide (What Every Reading Means)
